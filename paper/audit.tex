\paragraph{Expansion and growth test.}
A cosmological fit requires a specified $H(z)$, the corresponding density evolution, perturbation equations and the observables used by each dataset. An arbitrary late-time step in $H(z)$ is not supplied by the exact present-day multiplier. A transition rule must satisfy the chosen field equations and matching conditions. The exact relation $H_0^{\rm late}S_8^{\rm obs}=H_0^{\rm early}S_8^{\rm input}$ is a constraint on that proposed readout, not a guarantee of improved evidence.

\paragraph{Primordial and particle-sector consistency.}
The CP-to-baryon conversion must use an identified CP invariant, not whichever numerical input gives a closer comparison. The full CKM ansatz and the older geometric formula currently disagree, and the matrix-based conversion gives the larger residual. The proposed neutrino floor cannot be imposed as a successful joint cosmological/oscillation prior while its common-scale propagation spectrum fails the upstream splitting tests. Both applications need physical evolution laws before a likelihood interpretation.

\paragraph{Strong-field tests.}
The local saturation formulas can guide a model, but an exterior metric and operational observables are necessary before quoting neutron-star deviations or claiming a horizon. Reversible finite storage constrains information handling; it does not demonstrate radiation, evaporation or information recovery. Those claims require a state and update model across the proposed boundary.
