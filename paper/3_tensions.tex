\subsection{Expansion-rate rescaling}
Under the stated load rule,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
H_0^{\rm late}&=H_0^{\rm early}\mathcal L\\
&=(67.4\ \mathrm{km/s/Mpc})\frac{1491}{1376}\\
&\approx73.03\ \mathrm{km/s/Mpc}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The unrounded result is $73.032994186\ldots$ km/s/Mpc. Treating $\mathcal L$ as exact, the draft early-rate uncertainty $0.5$ propagates to $0.541787791\ldots$ km/s/Mpc. This accounts only for uncertainty in that input, not for uncertainty in the load rule or cosmological inference.

\subsection{Clustering rescaling}
Keeping the paper's inverse-load rule requires updating the clustering number at the same time:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
S_8^{\rm obs}&=\frac{S_8^{\rm input}}{\mathcal L}
=\frac{0.832}{1491/1376}\\
&=0.767828303\ldots.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The draft input uncertainty $0.013$ becomes $0.011997317\ldots$ under this exact-factor rescaling. The older value $0.77197$ used the two-term load and must not be retained with the revised Hubble result.

\subsection{A joint consistency constraint}
The same multiplier in the two rules gives the exact relation
\begin{equation}
H_0^{\rm late}S_8^{\rm obs}
=H_0^{\rm early}S_8^{\rm input}.
\end{equation}
This is proved in Lean. Consequently, changing a common load factor to improve one comparison also changes the other; the two outputs cannot be tuned independently. The draft central late targets do not satisfy this equality exactly, although quoted uncertainties must be considered before any statistical conclusion. A joint cosmological likelihood, with a specified expansion and growth history, is needed to test the rule as a physical model.
